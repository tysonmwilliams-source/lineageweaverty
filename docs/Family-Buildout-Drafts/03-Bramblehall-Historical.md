## The Legend of the "Ten Daughters"

In truth, Lord Constann fathered six daughters — not ten. The inflation came naturally over generations: the daughters of Bramblehall travelled with retainers and companions, many of them beautiful women in their own right. In the retelling, the serving women became daughters, the companions became sisters, and six became ten. Stories change. Details shift over the years. The legend is now so established that correcting it seems pointless — and the Bramblehall people see no reason to.

The six daughters' marriages served as a diplomatic web, binding Bramblehall to the other Wilfrey seats and allied houses. Their beauty became legend across all four seats and beyond. Unlike the Wilson lineage where daughters married into a NEW network of Crown houses, Constann's daughters married into the EXISTING Wilfrey power structure — strengthening Bramblehall's position within the established order.

**Contrast with House Wilson**: Where Wilson built an external network from scratch (10 Northern Crown Houses), Bramblehall's six daughters wove themselves into the Wilfrey web itself. This is the fundamental difference between the two broken branches — Wilson looked outward, Bramblehall looked inward.

## The Woodland Marriage Tradition (CANON)

- [ ] YES - Establish as codex entry:

```
**The Woodland Marriage Tradition** is the long-standing custom among the people of [[Bramblehall]] of marrying within the three woodland cadet branches: [[House Wilfbauer]], [[House Wilfbole]], and [[House Wilfbark]].

## The Tradition

Among the broader Bramblehall population — common folk, minor knights, cadet branch members — marrying within the woodland is the norm rather than the exception. The forest is vast, the communities close-knit, and the outside world far away. For most Bramblehall people, a spouse from another woodland family is the natural and expected choice.

## The Heir's Exception

For the lord's heir, however, the expectation has traditionally been different. An heir is expected to marry politically — to strengthen alliances with the other Wilfrey seats or with sworn houses. When a lord's heir DOES marry woodland, it is noteworthy and unusual, even at Bramblehall.

In nine generations of Bramblehall lords, only four have married woodland brides:
- **Aldwin** (Gen 3, ~1825) — married Mirette Wilfbark, the first heir to break convention
- **Aldric II** (Gen 6, ~1918) — married Branelisse Wilfbark
- **Bors** (Gen 7, ~1948) — married Woodline Wilfbauer
- **Alec** (Gen 8, ~1975) — married Beraline Wilfbole

The clustering of three consecutive woodland marriages in the modern era (Aldric II, Bors, Alec) is unprecedented and a source of quiet comment among the other seats.

## Advantages

- Deep loyalty from the woodland cadet branches
- Preservation of old traditions and forest knowledge
- Strong internal cohesion — the Bramblehall people are more closely related than those of any other seat

## Concerns

- Limited genetic diversity among the lordly line (when woodland marriages occur)
- Political isolation from the other Wilfrey seats
- Bramblehall is less connected to the wider Wilfrey network than Breakmount, Fourhearth, or Riverhead

## The Broader Pattern

While the lord's marriage choice attracts attention, the true strength of the tradition lies in the hundreds of marriages among the common folk, cadet branch members, and minor knights of the Bramblehall woodlands. These marriages — unremarked upon and unremarkable — are the fabric of Bramblehall's insular character.
```

While Lord Constann's daughters became legendary for their beauty, the famed "ten daughters" is an inflation of history.
